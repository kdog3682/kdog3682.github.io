The culture I grew up in is one of <Chinese text="努力" /> and <Chinese text="心苦" />. Between peers and parents, there are glorified comparisons: who is the most hard-working? Who has given the greatest effort? Mom and Dad <Chinese text="心苦" />, so the child must <Chinese text="努力" />.

"Grind", "persistence", and "hard work".

Lately though, possibly for the past 5 years, whenever I hear the words "grind", "blood, sweat, and tears", and "hard work", my guard instantly goes up. I no longer believe in these words. When I watch masters perform their craft, it never seems like grinding. They are completely immersed. They are flowing. When they are finished, there is no blood and no tears. There is contentment, the feeling of a job well done.

My friend Hai is a baker. He's a young guy. <Chinese text="师傅" /> is a word associated with elderly masters. Hai is referred to as Hai <Chinese text="师傅" />. He is the head baker for a small bakery.

He works from 5:30AM (or 7AM) to 3PM, 6 days a week, with an hour in the middle for lunch and some smoke breaks. Otherwise, his movements are non-stop, one action leading into the next. Everything is smooth and gentle, yet in the gentleness there is considerable strength. There is only one word to describe what Hai <Chinese text="师傅" /> looks like when he works: he is flowing.

Someone watching Hai <Chinese text="师傅" /> would say, "Wow, he works hard." For him, that's just how work is done. Anything less is simply not the way.
